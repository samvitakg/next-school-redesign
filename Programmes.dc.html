Key features

01

Autonomous learning

Montessori is a revolutionary program in the sense that it’s the only program that allows for a very high degree of autonomy and freedom, even with children as young as 1.5 years of age!

02

Developing independence

From a very young age, children are making their own decisions about what to do, when to do it and also developing an ability to do things by themselves.

03

Personalisation

Montessori education values the unique abilities of each child, allowing them to advance at their own pace without unnecessary competition, fostering intrinsic motivation and self-discipline.

04

Holistic learning

Montessori education is designed to be sensorial. Montessori materials help children refine their senses. Colour tablets, sound cylinders, and texture boards are used to enhance sensory perception and cognitive skills.

Key skills

Self-care

Washing, dressing, toileting, and eating, according to each child’s individual capacity.

Care of the environment

Cleaning, food preparation and food service; plant care and animal care.

Large-motor activities

Walking, climbing, running, jumping, balancing, climbing steps, and more.

Fine-motor skills

Reaching, grasping, picking up objects, transferring objects, using tools and utensils, doing art work.

Language

Naming objects, describing actions and intentions, discussing pictures, conversation, music, and singing.

Social skills

Developing skills through interactions with peers, advisors.

NEXT Program · Nursery to Grade 12

The IB continuum, the Big Picture way

We are an IB World School offering a continuum of all four programmes – the Primary Years Programme (PYP), Middle Years Programme (MYP), Diploma Programme (DP) and Career-related Programme (CP).

At next, we follow the Big Picture philosophy and methodology and this remains consistent across all our programs. At a minimum, around 50% of the time is spent in the ‘Big Picture mode’. How the balance time is spent is determined by the program and the academic load you choose for your child.

Free to Learn · 7 yrs & above

The Free to Learn program

Free to learn is a specialised program at Next that does away with mandatory academics.

At Next we follow the revolutionary Big Picture philosophy, but because of the requirements of the IB, only around 50% of the time is spent in the ‘Big Picture’ mode and the balance 50% is still focused on traditional academics.

But in the Free to Learn program, the idea is that students will spend 100% of the time in the ‘Big Picture’ mode, which is what we truly believe in. This is only possible if we completely remove mandatory academics, so this program is only for parents who are willing to take that plunge.

Basics

There are hundreds of schools around the world that have done away with academic mandates, and the students easily pick up the basics of reading, writing, maths at their own pace.

Future prospects

When the students are in 8th grade, they will have options to pursue the IB MYP program or give the NIOS exams.

NEXT Plus · Learners with diverse needs

The NEXT Plus program

Next Plus is a specialised program at Next School that’s created for children with diverse needs like Autism Spectrum Disorder, Intellectual and Learning Disorders, ADHD and other conditions. We’re currently open to learners from age 6 to 12.

Next School is bringing together its revolutionary Big Picture program with a functional learning program to help learners with diverse needs become independent and integrate into the real world.

The two pieces

Big Picture

Big Picture is the revolutionary program we run at Next that focuses on real world learning and learning by doing. It is completely personalised for each child.

One on one sessions

One on one sessions with the advisor help unearth the unique passions and interests of each student and also help address personal challenges.

Passion projects

The advisors help each student take up real world projects based on their interests and also helps set goals, track progress and give feedback.

Internships

The internship program helps older students integrate into the real world and figure out what kind of work they will enjoy based on their interests.

Functional learning

Functional learning skills are focused on helping the student be independent and also learn to work in and contribute to society effectively.

Experiential learning

Learning literacy and numeracy not through the traditional methods but through real world experiences.

Independent living

Developing skills in areas of hygiene, personal care, cooking, managing finances, household management, travel and safety.
